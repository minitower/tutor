# The Idea Machine: A Sandbox Course for Kids Who've Never Coded (Ages 7–8+)

A course for kids who don't know any programming, built around one loop:
**write down your idea → the machine builds it → you play with it → you
make it better.** No syntax, no typing code — the "code" a kid writes is
a few simple sentences on a fill-in-the-blank "Idea Card." Claude Code
does the actual building.

This is the youngest sibling of [../ai-agent-dev-course](../ai-agent-dev-course/README.md)
(professional developers) and [../ai-agent-dev-course-teens](../ai-agent-dev-course-teens/README.md)
(teens 14–17). Same underlying loop — describe what you want clearly,
check what you got, make it better — but here it's a sandbox, not a
curriculum with technical vocabulary. There's no "spec" or "plan mode" by
name; there's an Idea Card and a magic-feeling machine that reads it.

**This also works for any total beginner, not just kids.** An adult who's
never touched a coding tool before can use the same Idea Cards and the
same loop — the fill-in-the-blank framing removes "I don't know where to
start," which is the real barrier for most first-timers, kid or grown-up.

## Who this is for

Kids around 7–8 who can read and write simple sentences with some help,
have never programmed, and want to see their ideas turn into real,
playable things on a screen. No prior computer skills assumed beyond
being comfortable typing short words with an adult nearby.

## How this works, mechanically

- **The kid types.** Short answers on simple templates (fill-in-the-blank
  "Idea Cards"), not free-form code or long paragraphs. See
  [SUPERVISOR-GUIDE.md](SUPERVISOR-GUIDE.md) for exactly how much support
  is expected at this age — an adult stays present the whole session,
  helps with spelling/reading, and handles anything beyond the Idea
  Card itself (approving Claude Code's actions, running the app so the
  kid can see it).
- **Every session is short.** 15–25 minutes. Attention span drives the
  pacing here more than content depth.
- **Every session ends with something the kid can actually play with or
  show someone.** This isn't a course that pays off at the end — each
  session pays off immediately.

## Format

- 11 sessions, progressive — each idea a bit bigger than the last: a
  picture with your name on it → a button that does something → picking
  your own colors → saying exactly what you mean → a story with choices
  → finding and fixing something silly that's wrong → showing it to
  someone → your own big idea, start to finish
- Each session file has two parts: **For You** (read aloud to or with the
  kid — simple, short) and **For the Grown-Up** (what's actually
  happening in Claude Code, and what to do)
- No written reflections or reports — checkpoints are "show someone" or
  "tell someone," matched to the age

## What a kid gets out of this

By the end, a kid can:
1. Turn an idea in their head into a few written sentences someone (or
   something) else can understand
2. Notice when something the machine made isn't quite what they meant,
   and say what's wrong instead of just accepting it
3. Understand, in their own words, that the "magic machine" is actually
   reading what they wrote and doing exactly that — not reading their
   mind
4. Have made several real, working, playable things themselves

## Open questions to settle before running this

- One kid one-on-one with a parent, or a small group/classroom setting
  with one adult per couple of kids?
- Does each kid keep their own running "Idea Book" (paper or digital) of
  every card they've filled out, as a keepsake?
- Is there a "show someone" moment planned with family/friends at the
  end (Session 9/7), or does it stay just within the session?
