# Facilitator Script — Session 5: Bug Hunt: Find What's Silly

Read the spoken lines out loud to the kid. **[Grown-Up action]** lines
are things you do, not say.

**[Grown-Up action]** Before the session starts, quietly ask Claude
Code to rebuild one of the kid's earlier ideas (a page, a button, a
colored page — anything they've made before) with one small thing
deliberately wrong: the wrong color, a button that does the wrong
thing, or a typo in the text. Don't tell the kid what it is, and don't
build it while they're watching.

---

## Slide 1 — Welcome back!

"Hey! Remember your story with choices from last time? Today we're
doing something a little different — and kind of fun."

---

## Slide 2 — Today: a Bug Hunt!

"Sometimes when you ask the machine for something, it gets a little
bit wrong — even grown-up programmers deal with this all the time!
Today we're going to go looking for something silly on purpose, like
detectives. Finding it is the fun part."

---

## Slide 3 — Your Idea Card

"I already asked the machine to make something, based on one of your
old ideas. Which one do you think it might be?"

**[Grown-Up action]** Fill in the "I asked for" blank on the Idea Card
together, naming which earlier session's idea got rebuilt. Leave the
other two blanks empty for now.

---

## Slide 4 — Uh-oh... look at this!

"Okay, here's what it made. Take a good, close look — click around if
you want."

**[Grown-Up action]** Open the result you secretly built before the
session. Let the kid look and click without saying anything about
what's wrong.

---

## Slide 5 — What's wrong? Let's say it!

"Does something look silly or not quite right to you?"

If they spot it right away: "Nice eye! Tell me exactly what's wrong."
If they get stuck, give a small hint — point *near* it, not at it —
rather than naming it outright. If they seem embarrassed or frustrated
for not seeing it fast: "This isn't a test — even grown-ups have to
hunt for these sometimes. Finding it is the whole game, not a mess-up."

**[Grown-Up action]** Once they've named it, fill in the "What's silly
or wrong about it" blank. Then ask, "So what should happen instead?"
and fill in the third blank.

---

## Slide 6 — Sending the fix

"Now let's tell the machine what should happen instead, so it can fix
it."

**[Grown-Up action]** Open Claude Code and type an instruction built
from the card, for example: *"In [the earlier thing], [what's wrong]
is wrong — it should be [what should happen instead]. Please fix it."*
Read the instruction out loud before sending it.

---

## Slide 7 — Watch it get fixed

"Watch — it's reading exactly what we told it."

**[Grown-Up action]** As Claude Code works, narrate what's happening
and approve its actions out loud, e.g., "it's changing the color now,
is that okay?"

---

## Slide 8 — Before → After

"Let's look at it again now that it's fixed."

**[Grown-Up action]** Open the updated result next to (or right after)
the old one, if you can, so the kid can compare them.

"See the difference? That's the fix you asked for."

---

## Slide 9 — You fixed a bug!

"You noticed something was wrong, said what it should be instead, and
now it's fixed. That's exactly what real programmers do all day — and
it's fun, right?"

If there was any frustration earlier, circle back to it here: "That
silly mistake wasn't a mess-up. Finding it was the good part."

---

## Slide 10 — You're a bug hunter!

"How did you know something was wrong? What made you notice it?"

Let the kid answer in their own words — that noticing is the real
skill today, more than the fix itself.

"Great work — you're officially a bug hunter now."
