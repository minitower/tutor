## Quick Rewind: Module 3

- You wrote a one-page plan before the agent touched any code
- Goal, Not This, How It Should Behave, Done Means
- It caught unclear thinking *before* it became wrong code
- Worked great — but it also took real time to write

<!-- Slide 4 -->

## The Big Idea

**Not everything needs a written plan.**

- For small stuff, or when you're still exploring an idea, talking it
  through step by step — correcting as you go — is faster
- The plan isn't the point. Getting a good result efficiently is the point
- Knowing *when to skip* the paperwork is its own skill

---

<!-- Slide 5 -->

## When Talking It Through Wins

- The task is small — you could describe it in one sentence
- You don't fully know what you want yet, and you'll figure it out by
  seeing what comes back
- It's disposable, or a quick experiment
- You're right there to notice drift and correct it immediately

---

<!-- Slide 6 -->

## When a Written Plan Still Wins

- The task is big, or has edge cases you can already predict
- You're going to keep working on this for weeks or months
- You won't be there to correct drift in real time
- Someone else needs to understand the "why" later, not just the "what"

---

<!-- Slide 7 -->

## What "Purely Conversational" Means

- No written plan, no doc, no upfront spec
- Just back-and-forth: ask for a piece, look at it, react, adjust
- Correct direction the moment you notice it drifting — don't wait
- Still deliberate. Just not written down first

## While You Work

- Keep a rough count: how many messages/turns did it take?
- Notice the moments where you had to say "actually, not like that"
- That's not failure — that's the correcting-as-you-go this approach
  is built on

## Checkpoint

Compare this to your Module 3 result:

- Which took more effort overall?
- Which result would you trust more for something you'll work on for
  months? Which was fine for something quick and disposable?

---

<!-- Slide 11 -->

## The "Should've Written This Down" Moment

- Some tasks start small and quietly grow mid-conversation
- Is there a point where you felt the plan should have existed after all?
- Learning to feel that moment *as it happens* is the real skill here

---

<!-- Slide 12 -->

## Recap

- Not everything needs a written plan — small or exploratory work often
  doesn't
- Talking it through: faster, more flexible, needs you paying attention
- Writing it down: slower upfront, pays off on bigger or longer-lived work
- The skill is choosing correctly, not picking one forever
