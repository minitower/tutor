# Module 12 — Capstone: Build & Show

## Big idea

Put everything together on one real project you actually want to
finish — not a toy exercise, the thing you've been building since
Module 3 (or a new one, if you want a fresh start).

## Format

1. **Spec it** (Module 3) — a real one-page plan: goal, not-this,
   important behavior, done-means
2. **Plan it** (Module 5) — have the agent propose its approach before
   any big changes; review before approving
3. **Test the core logic** (Module 6) — at least the part that actually
   matters, not just boilerplate
4. **Get it reviewed** (Module 8) — a fresh session, no memory of how it
   was built, checking the result against your spec
5. **Keep your design consistent** (Module 4) if it has any UI at all
6. Fix anything the review caught, and update your spec if it turned out
   to be wrong or incomplete anywhere

## Deliverable

- The finished project
- Your spec, plan, tests, and review notes as separate, saved artifacts
  — not just remembered, actually written down
- A short walkthrough (written or spoken, your choice) covering: what
  you built, what the agent did vs. what you did yourself, and one thing
  that surprised you across the whole course

## Self-check before you call it done

- Could someone else read your spec and understand what this was
  supposed to do, without you explaining it out loud?
- Did the actual build match the plan you approved — and if not, do you
  know why?
- Do your tests actually check the behavior that matters, or just the
  easy happy-path case?
- Did the review pass catch anything real, or did you rubber-stamp it?
- Is your walkthrough honest about what the agent did, per your own
  Module 11 policy?

## Supervisor note

This is the showcase moment if your program has one — standard
supervision level for the build itself, but worth being present for the
walkthrough, since that's the best signal for whether the understanding
actually stuck across the whole course.
