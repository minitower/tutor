# Module 14 — Rules of the Road

## Big idea

Using AI help to code is normal now — but *how* you use it, and whether
you're honest about it, matters. For a class assignment, a portfolio
piece you show to a college or a job, or just your own sense of what you
actually know how to do, the line between "I directed this well" and "I
don't actually understand what I turned in" is one you have to draw for
yourself, on purpose, before you're in a situation where it's easier not
to.

## Why it matters

Nobody else can fully police this line for you, and it gets more
important, not less, as the tools get better. Deciding your own rules
now — while the stakes are a school project, not a job — is much easier
than figuring it out under pressure later.

## Hands-on task

Write your own short "AI use policy," a page or less, covering:
- When you'll use an agent for a project, and when you'll deliberately
  do something yourself even if the agent could do it faster
- How you'll disclose AI help when it's relevant (a class project, a
  portfolio note, telling a teammate)
- What you'll never skip doing yourself, even if you're in a hurry —
  the reasoning/understanding part, not just the "does it run" part

## Checkpoint

The policy itself, plus one sentence next to each rule explaining *why*
it's there — not just what the rule is.

## Supervisor note

Standard supervision. Worth a real conversation, not just a written
exercise — ask what happens if a teacher explicitly bans AI use on an
assignment, and how that interacts with the policy they just wrote.
